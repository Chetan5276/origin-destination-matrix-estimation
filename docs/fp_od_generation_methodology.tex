\begin{abstract}
This document specifies a \emph{first-principles} synthetic origin--destination (OD) generator that does \textbf{not} require a historical OD survey matrix. The process samples low-dimensional latent production and attraction factors, builds a gravity interaction kernel with distance decay and approximate reciprocity, applies Gamma heavy-tailed corridor weights, draws Dirichlet compositional noise, scales demand to a fixed total $G$, and balances marginals with iterative proportional fitting (IPF). Dataset-level quality filters enforce diversity, sparsity, and trip-length realism. The pipeline is implemented separately from the base-OD Dirichlet generator under \texttt{src/data/first\_principles/}. Turning counts reuse the existing Phase~2 assignment pipeline. We detail each stage, distributional choices, alternatives considered, and pros/cons.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Motivation and Problem Framing}
\label{ch:fp-motivation}

\section{Why first principles?}

Machine-learning OD estimators need large labeled datasets. Two strategies exist:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Perturb a survey OD} (existing pipeline): Dirichlet around $T^{\mathrm{base}}+\varepsilon(d)$, then IPF.
    \item \textbf{Synthesize from network geometry and latent demand} (this pipeline): no survey matrix required.
\end{enumerate}

The second strategy matches the problem statement in \texttt{od\_generation\_problem.pdf}: inputs are TAZs, distances, total demand $G$, and sample count $K$ only. It also forces the generator to encode transportation structure explicitly (gravity, reciprocity, latent land-use) rather than inheriting it from one observed table.

Not every constraint in the problem statement is a hard equality. We partition them as follows.

\begin{table}[ht]

\small

\toprule
\textbf{Type} & \textbf{Constraints} & \textbf{Enforcement} \\
\midrule
Hard (per matrix) &
$T_{ij}\ge 0$, $T_{ii}=0$, $\sum T=G$, $P$--$A$ consistency, connectivity support &

Soft (distributional) &
Distance decay, heavy tails, reciprocity, spatial coherence, corridor dominance, low-rank latent structure &

Trying to enforce reciprocity, freely varying corridors, and arbitrary asymmetric marginals as \emph{exact} hard constraints simultaneously is over-specified. Soft enforcement in expectation is the scientifically correct reading of the problem note.

\chapter{Stage A: Latent Production and Attraction Factors}
\label{ch:fp-latent}

\section{Model}

Real OD tables are low-rank in spirit: a few land-use / activity types (residence, employment, retail, \ldots) drive zone importance. We therefore sample:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item City-level type intensities $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\mathrm{prod}},\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\mathrm{attr}}\in\mathbb{R}_+^{r}$,
    \[
      \pi^{\mathrm{prod}}_t,\ \pi^{\mathrm{attr}}_t \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(a_f,b_f).
    \]
    \item Soft zone--type memberships $\mathbfM\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}$ with rows on the simplex, obtained by placing $r$ random type centers in the plane and softmax-weighting zones by distance to centers.
    \item Zone factors
    \[
      u_i = \sum_{t=1}^{r} M_{it}\,\pi^{\mathrm{prod}}_t, \qquad
      v_j = \sum_{t=1}^{r} M_{jt}\,\pi^{\mathrm{attr}}_t.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Spatial coherence.}
Nearby zones should not have wildly unrelated importance. We smooth
\[
  \mathbfu \leftarrow (1-s)\, \mathbfu + s\, \mathbf{K}\mathbfu,
\]
where $K_{ij}\propto\exp(-d_{ij}^2/(2\lambda_s^2))$ is a row-normalized Gaussian kernel, and likewise for $\mathbfv$. Strength $s\in[0,1]$ and length $\lambda_s$ are hyperparameters (defaults $s=0.5$, $\lambda_s=400\,\mathrm{m}$).

\section{Why this design?}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Low-dimensional manifold:} the map $(M,\pi)\mapsto (u,v)\mapsto S$ has effective dimension $\ll N^2$, matching the problem note's latent-structure requirement.
    \item \textbf{Interpretable:} $r$ plays the role of ``number of activity types.''
    \item \textbf{No survey OD:} factors are drawn from the network geometry and random centers only.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Stage B: Gravity Kernel and Reciprocity}
\label{ch:fp-gravity}

\section{Gravity interaction}

Given $\mathbfu,\mathbfv$ and impedance $f(d)$,
\begin{equation}
  S_{ij} = u_i\, v_j\, f(d_{ij})\, \mathbbm{1}[i\neq j].
  \label{eq:fp-gravity}
\end{equation}
Supported impedance families:
\begin{align}
  f_{\mathrm{exp}}(d) &= \mathrm{e}^{-d/\lambda}, \\
  f_{\mathrm{pow}}(d) &= (d+\varepsilon)^{-\gamma}.
\end{align}
Default: exponential with $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{m}$ (order of median Sioux Falls inter-zone distance).

\paragraph{Why gravity?}
It is the classical transportation interaction model: productions, attractions, and distance decay enter multiplicatively. In expectation, longer pairs receive less mass---the soft distance-decay constraint---while remaining strictly positive for finite $d$.

\section{Approximate reciprocity}

Daily demand is often roughly two-way. We mix
\begin{equation}
  S \leftarrow (1-\rho)\,S + \rho\, S^\top, \qquad \rho\in[0,0.5].
  \label{eq:fp-reciprocity}
\end{equation}
Default $\rho=0.35$. This is \emph{not} hard symmetry: Gamma weights and Dirichlet noise later break exact equality $T_{ij}=T_{ji}$, preserving statistical reciprocity.

\chapter{Stage C: Heavy-Tailed Corridor Weights}
\label{ch:fp-gamma}

\section{Model}

Gravity alone tends to be too smooth. Empirical OD cells are skewed. We reweight
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\gamma,b_\gamma), \qquad
  S_{ij}\leftarrow w_{ij} S_{ij}\quad (i\neq j),
\end{equation}
with density
\begin{equation}
  f(w)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_\gamma)b_\gamma^{a_\gamma}} w^{a_\gamma-1}\mathrm{e}^{-w/b_\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Default $a_\gamma=0.5<1$: density diverges as $w\to 0^+$ and retains a long right tail $\Rightarrow$ many tiny OD pairs, few dominant corridors. Corridor \emph{identity} changes every sample via independent draws---random corridor dominance.

\chapter{Stage D: Dirichlet Compositional Noise}
\label{ch:fp-dirichlet}

\section{Model}

After Stages B--C, normalize $\mathbfS$ to a probability matrix $\mathbfp^{\mathrm{prior}}$ on off-diagonal support and draw
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbfp} \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}\!\bigl(\alpha\,\mathbfp^{\mathrm{prior}}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Mean $\mathbb{E}[\tilde{p}]=\mathbfp^{\mathrm{prior}}$; coordinate variance scales as $1/(\alpha+1)$. Default $\alpha=200$ (somewhat more diverse than the base-OD pipeline's $\alpha=500$, because the gravity skeleton already carries structure).

\paragraph{Why Dirichlet?}
Trip tables are compositional under fixed $G$. The Dirichlet is the natural distribution on the simplex. Independent Gaussian cell noise would violate the simplex or require awkward renormalization.

\chapter{Stage E: Scaling and IPF}
\label{ch:fp-ipf}

\section{Hard constraints}

Set seed $X_{ij}=G\,\tilde{p}_{ij}$ (plus a tiny floor on support for IPF stability). Marginal targets:
\begin{equation}
  P_i = G\,\frac{u_i}{\sum_{i'}u_{i'}}, \qquad
  A_j = G\,\frac{v_j}{\sum_{j'}v_{j'}},
\end{equation}
or equal shares if \texttt{--equal-marginals}. IPF (Furness) alternates row/column scaling until marginal error $<\tau$ or $t_{\max}$ iterations. Post-process: zero diagonal and off-support.

This enforces:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item nonnegativity (up to numerical tolerance),
    \item $T_{ii}=0$,
    \item $\sum_{i,j}T_{ij}=G$ (to numerical accuracy),
    \item $P$--$A$ consistency with the chosen targets,
    \item connectivity support (full off-diagonal for connected zoning systems such as Sioux Falls).
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Stage F: Dataset Quality Filters}
\label{ch:fp-filters}

\section{Procedure}

Generate a candidate pool of size $\lceil\kappa K\rceil$ (default $\kappa=2$), then accept $K$ matrices using the shared module \texttt{quality\_filters.py}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item sparsity band;
    \item trip-length relative error vs.\ a \emph{reference} gravity OD (deterministic latent draw, no Gamma/Dirichlet);
    \item optional correlation band vs.\ that reference (defaults wide: disabled);
    \item entropy-oriented ranking;
    \item pairwise Frobenius diversity (reservoir approximation for scalability).
\end{itemize}

The reference OD is \textbf{not} a survey matrix; it is an internal gravity template used only for filter geometry checks.

\section{Scientific rationale}

The generator is a \emph{hierarchical probabilistic model}:
\[
  (\mathbfu,\mathbfv)
  \to \mathbfS_{\mathrm{grav}}
  \to \mathbfS_{\mathrm{heavy}}
  \to \mathbfp
  \to \mathbf{T}=\mathrm{IPF}(G\mathbfp).
\]
Each arrow adds a transportation mechanism that the ML inverse problem should eventually recover from turning counts. Because structure is injected by geometry and latents---not by cloning a single survey---the dataset explores a realistic manifold of OD tables while remaining on-policy for the problem statement.

\chapter{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{ch:fp-limits}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Membership centers are geometric proxies, not calibrated land-use layers.
    \item Reciprocity is a linear mix, not a structural home--work matching model.
    \item Connectivity currently uses full off-diagonal support (valid for fully connected zone systems); sparse network cut-sets could be added.
    \item Hyperparameters $(r,\lambda,\rho,\alpha,a_\gamma)$ still need network-specific calibration via held-out realism metrics.
    \item Optional: replace Euclidean $d_{ij}$ with network shortest-path costs for impedance.
\end{itemize}

\chapter*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}

The first-principles pipeline generates OD matrices from zones, distances, and total demand alone by combining latent land-use factors, gravity interaction, Gamma corridor noise, Dirichlet compositional sampling, IPF balancing, and dataset filters. Each stage targets a subset of the constraints in \texttt{od\_generation\_problem.pdf}. The implementation lives beside---not instead of---the existing base-OD generator, and turning counts reuse Phase~2 unchanged.
